\begin{abstract}
This addendum specifies \Pair{}, a versioned comparison receipt over two independently constructed 15-coordinate Market Field instances. The operator enforces a common component recipe, exact timestamp intersection, bilateral coordinate support, independent proper-fit normalization, full-precision relative-price context, a strictly prior rolling beta adjustment, ordered provenance, and zero decision authority. We give the alignment key, availability masks, native and fit-relative differences, relative-index and beta-adjusted-return equations, family-balanced relationship-stretch rule, precision boundaries, receipt hash, algebraic invariants, failure modes, and test map needed to reconstruct the implementation. The method does not select peers, estimate cointegration, infer connectedness, identify a winner, or establish predictive or economic value. Its contribution is an auditable comparison and translation layer, not a relative-value trading model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Technical Summary}

\Pair{} answers a deliberately narrow question: given two instruments analyzed by the same Market Field recipe, what can be compared without hiding timestamp mismatch, unsupported coordinates, self-history normalization, or downstream authority? It produces three parallel descriptive channels:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a normalized relative-price path;
    \item a prior-only beta-adjusted log-return path; and
    \item native-scale and own-history-relative gaps for each of the 15 field coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
The price channels do not transform the field coordinates. The coordinate channels do not alter either component dictionary, match cross-symbol Form identifiers, or supply a trade score.

\textbf{Supported claim.} Pair v1 integrates exact alignment, bilateral support, independent fixed-reference normalization, ordered identity, and explicit zero authority in one reconstructible receipt. \textbf{Unsupported claims.} It is not evidence of alpha, mean reversion, market neutrality, factor neutrality, leadership, spillovers, causal propagation, prediction, option efficacy, or profitability.

\end{center}

\section{Relationship to Established Methods}

Benchmark-relative returns and market-model slopes are established tools. Brown and Warner study event-window abnormal-return methods using estimated return models \citep{brownwarner1985daily}. Pair v1 borrows only the familiar covariance/variance slope form. It defines no event, estimates no event-window effect, performs no cross-sectional inference, and does not subtract a fitted intercept from its displayed beta-adjusted return.

Likewise, normalized price distance and convergence rules are central to classical pairs-trading research \citep{gatevetal2006pairs}. Pair v1 has no pair-formation optimization, trading band, entry or exit rule, convergence assumption, transaction-cost model, or return claim. It also performs no unit-root, residual-stationarity, cointegrating-vector, or error-correction test of the type associated with cointegration analysis \citep{englegranger1987cointegration}. Finally, the coordinate gaps are not a variance-decomposition network: no VAR or forecast-error variance decomposition is estimated, so the output is not Diebold--Yilmaz connectedness \citep{dieboldyilmaz2012spillovers}.

The implementation contribution is therefore narrower: co-register two independently calculated multiscale fields, expose where both are actually supported, compare each instrument both natively and relative to its own fixed fit history, keep price context separate, and bind the result to an ordered receipt.

\section{Inputs, Coordinate Space, and Component Chronology}

\subsection{Independent component fields}

Let $q\in\{A,B\}$ denote the target and benchmark. Each leg begins with a normalized OHLCV history and is independently mapped by a component recipe $\mathcal R$ to
\begin{equation}
    \left\{X^q_{j,t},M^q_{j,t}\right\}_{j=1}^{15},
    \quad
    m^q_j,\ s^q_j,\ e_q,\ H_q ,
\end{equation}
where $X^q_{j,t}$ is coordinate $j$, $M^q_{j,t}$ is its full-dependency-support indicator, $m^q_j$ and $s^q_j$ are the stored proper-fit location and scale, $e_q$ is the evaluation start, and $H_q$ is the component analysis hash. The service requires equal recipe hashes. That equality binds the formula version, semantic revision, ordered horizon grid, settings, normalization contract, EWM convention, and retrospective-research inclusion flag. It does not assert equal histories or equal providers.

For requested visible length $V$ and largest horizon $h_{\max}$, each component asks the history layer for
\begin{equation}
    V_{\mathrm{fetch}}=V+\max(72,2h_{\max})
\end{equation}
rows and requires at least
\begin{equation}
    n_{\min}=\max(60,h_{\max}+1)
\end{equation}
returned rows. The field and its proper-fit/calibration/evaluation split are calculated on that retained component history. Pair output is subsequently restricted to the final $V$ common aligned observations. Consequently, earlier component rows may initialize the field and its dictionary but are not used by the visible-window relative-price base or beta estimator.

\section{Exact Alignment and Availability Contract}

\subsection{Alignment keys}

Let $s$ be a serialized component timestamp and $\tau$ the requested timeframe. Pair v1 constructs
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\tau(s)=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{date}(s), & \tau\in\{\texttt{1D},\texttt{1W}\},\\
\operatorname{ISO}(\operatorname{UTC}(s)), &
    \tau\notin\{\texttt{1D},\texttt{1W}\},\ s\text{ timezone-aware},\\
\operatorname{ISO}(s), &
    \tau\notin\{\texttt{1D},\texttt{1W}\},\ s\text{ timezone-naive}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:key}
\end{equation}
If $K_q$ is leg $q$'s valid key set, define
\begin{align}
K^{\mathrm{all}}_{AB} &=
    \operatorname{sort}(K_A\cap K_B),\\
K_{AB} &= \tail_V(K^{\mathrm{all}}_{AB}).
\label{eq:intersection}
\end{align}
The explicit window counts are
\begin{align}
N_{\mathrm{requested}}&=V,\\
N_{\mathrm{available}}&=|K^{\mathrm{all}}_{AB}|,\\
N_{\mathrm{returned}}&=|K_{AB}|
=\min(V,N_{\mathrm{available}}).
\label{eq:window-counts}
\end{align}
The response also returns $|K_A|$, $|K_B|$, the first and last returned keys,
and whether $N_{\mathrm{available}}>N_{\mathrm{returned}}$. These counts
separate the requested display window from what exact alignment made available.
At least 20 exact shared observations must exist before the visible tail is selected. No nearest-neighbor match, interpolation, forward fill, or carry-forward is permitted.

Daily and weekly dates are provider-serialized date labels, not an independent exchange-calendar certification. Aware intraday times are normalized to UTC before exact equality; naive intraday times remain naive and require exact serialized equality. A matching key does not prove the two observations describe the same economic session. Session compatibility is reported as \texttt{compatible} only for the same canonical symbol and otherwise remains \texttt{unknown}.

\subsection{Visible-window dropout accounting}

Let $k_0=\min K_{AB}$ and $k_T=\max K_{AB}$. For leg $q$, the displayed dropped count is
\begin{equation}
D_q=\#\left\{k\in K_q:k\ge k_0,\ k\notin K_{AB}\right\},
\label{eq:dropped}
\end{equation}
and the unmatched tail count is
\begin{equation}
U_q=\#\left\{k\in K_q:k>k_T\right\}.
\label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
Thus $U_q$ is a subset of $D_q$ and must not be added to it. These quantities do not count pre-window truncation or rows rejected before the pair layer during OHLCV normalization. The receipt reports counts, not the complete list of dropped timestamps.

\subsection{Coordinate support}

For each leg, $M^q_{j,t}=1$ only when the coordinate has reached its declared first full-dependency-support timestamp and the serialized value is finite. Scaling exponent additionally requires its validity flag. Each carrier additionally requires a finite corresponding causal-ratio observation. Define bilateral support
\begin{equation}
S_{j,t}=
M^A_{j,t}M^B_{j,t}
\mathbf 1\!\left[X^A_{j,t},X^B_{j,t}\ \text{finite}\right].
\label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
The support fraction in the response is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{SupportFraction}
=\frac{C_{\mathrm{supported}}}{C_{\mathrm{total}}},
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{supported}}
=\sum_{t\in K_{AB}}\sum_{j=1}^{15}S_{j,t},
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{total}}=15N_{\mathrm{returned}}.
\label{eq:support-fraction}
\end{equation}
The response reports both cell counts, their ratio, the literal bilateral
full-dependency-support rule, whether every returned cell is supported, and
\texttt{missing\_values\_carried=false}. This is visible-window
native-coordinate support. It is neither latest-coordinate support, context
support, nor a price-overlap fraction.

\subsection{DXY routing boundary}

Requested symbols \texttt{DXY}, \texttt{\^{}DXY}, and \texttt{DX-Y.NYB} canonicalize to \texttt{DXY} and retain Yahoo's provider symbol \texttt{DX-Y.NYB}; the system does not silently substitute the tradable ETF \texttt{UUP}. Because the current provider bar anchors are not demonstrated compatible, nonidentity DXY comparisons at \texttt{1h}, \texttt{2h}, and \texttt{4h} are rejected before provider access. DXY/DXY remains available as an identity control. Permitting another timeframe is not a claim that its sessions are economically synchronized.

\section{Coordinate Comparison Operators}

\subsection{Native difference}

The native operator is
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{N}_{j,t}=
\begin{cases}
X^A_{j,t}-X^B_{j,t}, & S_{j,t}=1,\\
\NA, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:native}
\end{equation}
An unavailable cell remains unavailable; it is never interpreted as a zero difference.

\subsection{Own-history fit-relative difference}

For completeness, the component dictionary obtains $m^q_j$ from the coordinate median on its proper-fit segment. Its robust scale is selected deterministically:
\begin{equation}
s^q_j=
\begin{cases}
Q_{0.75}-Q_{0.25}, & Q_{0.75}-Q_{0.25}>10^{-9},\\
1.4826\,\med|X-m^q_j|, & \text{if that value}>10^{-9},\\
\operatorname{Std}_{\mathrm{pop}}(X), & \text{if that value}>10^{-9},\\
1, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:robust-scale}
\end{equation}
All quantities in Equation~\ref{eq:robust-scale} use that leg's proper-fit rows. $Q_{0.25}$ and $Q_{0.75}$ use NumPy \texttt{method=linear} interpolation. Pair v1 consumes the stored fit references rather than recomputing them. A stored scale must be finite and greater than $10^{-12}$.

Define
\begin{equation}
Z^q_{j,t}=\frac{X^q_{j,t}-m^q_j}{s^q_j}.
\label{eq:z}
\end{equation}
The context difference is
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{C}_{j,t}=
\begin{cases}
Z^A_{j,t}-Z^B_{j,t},
& S_{j,t}=1,\ t\ge\max(e_A,e_B),\\
\NA,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:context}
\end{equation}
It compares how unusual the two coordinates are relative to their \emph{separate} proper-fit histories. It is not a cross-sectional economic z-score. Fit and held-out calibration rows are not backfilled with later reference statistics, and request-local Form identifiers or centroids are never matched between symbols.

\subsection{Precision boundary}

Pair-v1 field gaps operate on the public component receipt. Coordinate series values enter from the component's four-decimal serialization; fit medians and robust scales enter from its six-decimal lexicon serialization. Each $Z^q_{j,t}$ is serialized to six decimals before $\Delta^C_{j,t}$ is formed, and returned gaps are serialized to six decimals. These conventions make the receipt reconstructible from the component payloads but mean that the coordinate calculus is not a full-precision recomputation.

Price comparison follows a separate path. The service retains full-precision normalized closes privately beside each component analysis, computes relative price and beta adjustment from those closes, and only then serializes returned closes to eight decimals and return quantities to six decimals.

\subsection{Chronology and request-window dependence}

For a fixed request, native values are prefix-derived and context begins only after both legs' fixed proper-fit and calibration segments. Yet the context trace is not invariant to changing the request history: a different retained window can move split locations and change $m^q_j$ or $s^q_j$. Likewise, changing $V$ changes the first visible common close and therefore rebases the relative index. These are request-relative diagnostics, not persistent cross-symbol coordinates.

\section{Relative Price and Prior-Only Beta Adjustment}

\subsection{Normalized relative price}

Index the visible common keys as $t_0<\cdots<t_{n-1}$ and let $p^q_i>0$ be the full-precision close of leg $q$ at $t_i$. The relative index and active progress are
\begin{align}
RI_i&=100
\frac{p^A_i/p^A_0}{p^B_i/p^B_0},
\label{eq:ri}\\
AP_i&=RI_i-100.
\label{eq:active}
\end{align}
The index is a rebased price ratio. It does not control currency, dividends, corporate actions, or provider adjustment policy beyond what is present in the returned component histories.

\subsection{Strictly prior slope}

Define aligned log returns
\begin{equation}
r^q_i=\log\frac{p^q_i}{p^q_{i-1}},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n-1 .
\end{equation}
For current return $i$, the strictly prior estimation set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal W_i=
\left\{\max(1,i-60),\ldots,i-1\right\},
\qquad N_i=|\mathcal W_i|.
\label{eq:beta-window}
\end{equation}
No estimate is available unless $N_i\ge20$. With
\begin{equation}
\bar r^q_i=\frac{1}{N_i}\sum_{u\in\mathcal W_i}r^q_u,
\end{equation}
define
\begin{align}
V^B_i&=\sum_{u\in\mathcal W_i}(r^B_u-\bar r^B_i)^2,\\
\sigma^B_i&=\sqrt{\frac{V^B_i}{N_i}},\\
\widehat\beta^-_i&=
\frac{\sum_{u\in\mathcal W_i}
(r^A_u-\bar r^A_i)(r^B_u-\bar r^B_i)}
{V^B_i}.
\label{eq:beta}
\end{align}
This is the centered covariance/variance slope, equivalent to the slope from an intercept-inclusive OLS fit. The fitted intercept is neither returned nor subtracted below.

The estimate is unavailable if $N_i<20$, the two prior arrays differ in length, $\sigma^B_i<10^{-7}$, $\widehat\beta^-_i$ is nonfinite, or $|\widehat\beta^-_i|>25$. Equality at the two numerical thresholds is accepted. Estimates outside the gate are rejected, not clipped.

\subsection{Beta-adjusted chain}

The current increment is
\begin{equation}
e_i=r^A_i-\widehat\beta^-_i r^B_i.
\label{eq:beta-adjusted}
\end{equation}
Because Equation~\ref{eq:beta-adjusted} omits a fitted intercept, $e_i$ is a \emph{beta-adjusted log-return differential}, not an OLS residual, abnormal return, or alpha. Let $C_i$ be the cumulative log differential:
\begin{equation}
C_i=
\begin{cases}
\NA, & \widehat\beta^-_i\text{ unavailable},\\
e_i, & \widehat\beta^-_i\text{ available and }C_{i-1}\text{ unavailable},\\
C_{i-1}+e_i, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
The displayed cumulative value is
\begin{equation}
BA_i=100\{\exp(C_i)-1\}.
\label{eq:ba}
\end{equation}
An unavailable beta resets the chain. A later valid beta starts a new contiguous chain, and the latest summary never carries a stale estimate. Every price row reports the number of prior return pairs, nullable chain identifier, chain-start marker, and reset marker. The current summary separately reports the configured and realized prior-return counts, current-chain start and end, current-chain observation count, total chain and reset counts, and last-reset timestamp. Because the estimator is built only from visible common returns, its first possible value requires 20 prior returns plus the current return---22 aligned prices including the base row. If common keys contain time gaps, the corresponding log returns span those gaps; no elapsed-time normalization is applied.

\section{Family-Balanced Relationship Stretch}

Let $t$ be the latest common key and $t^{-5}$ the key five common observations earlier. For family $f\in\{\text{pressure state},\text{field transform},\text{OHLCV carrier}\}$, define the common endpoint support
\begin{equation}
J_f=\left\{j:F(j)=f,\
\Delta^C_{j,t}\text{ and }\Delta^C_{j,t^{-5}}\text{ finite}\right\}.
\label{eq:stretch-support}
\end{equation}
Using the \emph{same} $J_f$ at both endpoints prevents support appearing or disappearing from masquerading as relationship movement. For $u\in\{t,t^{-5}\}$,
\begin{align}
G_{f,u}&=\frac{1}{|J_f|}
\sum_{j\in J_f}|\Delta^C_{j,u}|,\\
S_u&=\frac{1}{3}\sum_fG_{f,u}.
\label{eq:stretch}
\end{align}
The summary is unavailable if any $J_f$ is empty. With
\begin{equation}
\eta=\max(0.05,0.05S_{t^{-5}}),
\end{equation}
the label is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{StretchLabel}=
\begin{cases}
\text{widening}, & S_t>S_{t^{-5}}+\eta,\\
\text{converging}, & S_t<S_{t^{-5}}-\eta,\\
\text{mixed}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:stretch-label}
\end{equation}
The three families receive equal total weight despite containing 5, 7, and 3 coordinates. The response returns $S_t$, $S_{t^{-5}}$, their change, $\eta$, the lookback, and the counts of participating families and coordinates alongside the categorical value. The reader-facing translation maps \texttt{mixed} to ``No clear net change'' because the branch includes movement that remains within tolerance; it is not evidence of a statistical mixture. Stretch measures standardized separation magnitude, not direction or leadership.

\section{Algebraic and Software Invariants}

\paragraph{Identity control.}
If the canonical symbol and component analysis are identical, then every supported native and context gap is zero and $RI_i=100$. Where the beta gate is admissible and benchmark variance is positive, $\widehat\beta^-_i=1$ and the beta-adjusted increment is zero; a locally constant return window remains unavailable rather than being forced to zero. The receipt labels the pair as an identity control.

\paragraph{Swap behavior.}
For a fixed common key set and fixed component references,
\begin{equation}
\Delta^N_{AB,j,t}=-\Delta^N_{BA,j,t},
\qquad
\Delta^C_{AB,j,t}=-\Delta^C_{BA,j,t}.
\label{eq:antisymmetry}
\end{equation}
Before response rounding,
\begin{equation}
RI_{BA,i}=\frac{10000}{RI_{AB,i}}.
\label{eq:reciprocity}
\end{equation}
Thus active progress does not simply change sign. The rolling beta-adjusted paths are directional estimators and need not be antisymmetric. Absolute relationship stretch and bilateral support are swap-invariant.

\paragraph{Missingness preservation.}
Equations~\ref{eq:native} and \ref{eq:context} never zero-fill an unsupported coordinate. The context gap is unavailable before the shared evaluation boundary, and Equation~\ref{eq:chain} never carries a beta-adjusted chain across an invalid estimate.

\paragraph{Prior-only beta property.}
At row $i$, Equation~\ref{eq:beta} uses returns only through $i-1$. Mutating prices after any earlier row cannot change that row's beta or cumulative beta-adjusted path. This is a computational prefix property, not causal inference.

\paragraph{Ordered identity.}
Swapping target and benchmark changes the ordered receipt preimage unless the two leg identities coincide. A cryptographic digest is used as an identity receipt, not as proof that the provider supplied correct, immutable, or exchange-complete bars.

\section{Receipt, Cache, and Authority Contracts}

\subsection{Component and ordered comparison hashes}

Each component analysis hash has the form
\begin{align}
H^{\mathrm{recipe}}_q
&=\sha(\text{canonical recipe payload}),\\
H^{\mathrm{input}}_q
&=\sha(\text{normalized OHLCV rows}),\\
H_q&=\sha\!\left(
\{H^{\mathrm{recipe}}_q,H^{\mathrm{input}}_q\}
\right).
\end{align}
The ordered comparison preimage contains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pair schema \texttt{market\_field\_pair\_v1};
    \item ordered target canonical symbol, provider symbol, and $H_A$;
    \item ordered benchmark canonical symbol, provider symbol, and $H_B$;
    \item timeframe;
    \item alignment contract \texttt{pair\_alignment\_v1};
    \item normalization contract \texttt{native\_and\_fixed\_proper\_fit\_relative\_v1}; and
    \item $\sha(K_{AB})$ for the ordered visible common keys.
\end{itemize}
Canonical JSON uses sorted keys, compact separators, finite JSON numbers, ASCII escaping, UTF-8 bytes, canonical positive zero, and SHA-256. Requested aliases such as \texttt{DXY} versus \texttt{\^{}DXY} remain visible provenance but are not distinct comparison-hash fields after they resolve to the same canonical and provider symbols. Provider name and data-source labels are also not committed separately. The API cache key includes the requested aliases so an alias-specific provenance receipt is not served from a differently requested alias.

\subsection{Frozen result receipt and deterministic translation}

The comparison hash identifies the ordered calculation contract; a separate
\texttt{market\_field\_pair\_receipt\_v1} freezes the returned evidence needed
to preserve one observed result. Let $R^{-}$ denote a canonical JSON object
containing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the Pair and semantic schema identifiers, timeframe, and shared-window endpoint;
    \item requested, canonical, and provider identities for both legs, their analysis hashes, data-source labels, and latest aligned/returned closes;
    \item the requested, available, and returned exact shared-window counts, per-leg dropped counts, unmatched tails, and latest-returned timestamps;
    \item the complete ordered shared-key sequence, $\sha(K_{AB})$, and alignment contract;
    \item coordinate-cell support counts and the explicit no-carry rule;
    \item session, currency, adjustment, and timestamp-alignment disclosures;
    \item the latest relative-index, beta-chain, field-separation, and 15-coordinate values;
    \item the component, comparison, recipe, alignment, and normalization identities; and
    \item the identity-control flag and zero-authority contract.
\end{itemize}
The service returns
\begin{equation}
R=R^{-}\cup\{\texttt{receipt\_hash}:\sha(R^{-})\}.
\label{eq:frozen-receipt}
\end{equation}
The response header repeats this digest as
\texttt{X-Market-Weather-Receipt-Hash}. Recomputing Equation~\ref{eq:frozen-receipt}
detects mutation of the serialized receipt body; it does not certify provider
truth, bar completeness, economic validity, or independent custody. SHA-256 is
used here as an unkeyed checksum, not a digital signature or proof of origin.

A separate \texttt{pair\_summary\_v1} is a deterministic descriptive
translation of authoritative relative-progress, current beta-chain,
coordinate-gap, field-separation, support, and session fields. It introduces
no fitted score, peer choice, forecast, or authority. Its three notable
coordinate rows are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_t=
\operatorname{first}_3
\operatorname{sort}_{j:S_{j,t}=1,\ \Delta^C_{j,t}\ \mathrm{finite}}
\left(-|\Delta^C_{j,t}|,\ j\right),
\label{eq:summary-top-three}
\end{equation}
where recipe position $j$ is the deterministic tie-breaker. Summary prose uses
two-decimal display formatting while the numeric response retains its declared
serialization precision. The live URL preserves
the selector recipe and reruns with then-current data; exporting $R$ preserves
the current evidence boundary. Version 1 of $R$ does not bind the summary
wording, generation/cache metadata, caveat prose, full price path, or full
coordinate histories and is therefore not a complete chart-replay archive.

\subsection{Reader-facing evidence contract}

The interface labels the construction an \emph{Auditable field recipe}, not an
independently audited one. Its Overview reports the all-window ratio in
Equation~\ref{eq:support-fraction} as supported coordinate cells over total
coordinate cells and reports current bilateral coordinate support separately.
It also separates observed session status from independent session
certification. The normalized price panel names the exact common base date on
which both price indexes equal 100.

Comparator selectors show a descriptive reference category---broad-cap,
growth/technology-heavy, equal-weight, small-cap, dollar-index, sector, or
custom---together with ``User selected; suitability not evaluated.'' They do
not recommend or score a peer. The export control is labeled
\texttt{Export compact receipt} $\cdot$ \texttt{JSON}; its pre-export
description states that it preserves aligned keys, current measurements,
support, identities, disclosures, and authority, excludes full chart
histories, and is not digitally signed.

Visual identity is redundant rather than color-only: target, benchmark, and
difference use solid/circle, dashed/diamond, and dotted/square encodings,
respectively. Current scope points also differ in outline and size. Unsupported
intervals use a gap or hatch. Coordinate strips place signed microbars above or
below a centerline, encode magnitude through height and intensity, and retain a
latest-direction arrow. Beta-chain starts and restarts remain visible as
discontinuities and labeled markers. These are translation conventions and do
not change any Pair coordinate, support mask, or identity.

\subsection{Operational caching}

Raw histories use the persistent market-history cache. Pair calculations use a per-worker TTL/LRU analysis cache with single-flight coalescing and isolated response copies. The ordered symbols, provider object identities, timeframe, visible length, horizons, and settings participate in that operational key. Cache metadata reports whether the pair computation was a hit, miss, or coalesced wait and whether either leg called a provider on that request.

Cache status is not scientific evidence. A hit does not certify data freshness, session comparability, provider truth, or bar completion. The response \texttt{generated\_at} records calculation time and may predate a cache-hit request.

The request-local \texttt{market\_field\_pair\_runtime\_v1} object and
\texttt{Server-Timing} header separate cache lookup/wait/copy work from a
miss-owner build. On a build, measured stages distinguish each leg's history
access, field construction, price-row preparation, and pair assembly. Identity
controls disclose benchmark-leg reuse rather than inventing a second-leg
duration. The endpoint returns one JSON response, so the measured
handler-to-response-ready boundary excludes FastAPI JSON serialization,
middleware compression, network transfer, frontend rendering, and chart
interaction. Those values remain explicitly unavailable rather than being
filled with synthetic progressive stages. Runtime metadata is operational and
request-specific; it is excluded from both the ordered comparison hash and
Equation~\ref{eq:frozen-receipt}.

The browser separately emits a neutral \texttt{pair\_surface\_second\_frame}
diagnostic from component mount through two animation-frame callbacks. That
quantity is not labeled as paint, first useful summary, or chart
interactivity. It does not enter either analytical identity.

\section{Limitations and Robustness Risks}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No efficacy result.} There is no prospective predictive, decision-support, or cost-aware economic evaluation for Pair v1.
    \item \textbf{User-selected comparators.} Competitor, sector, SPY, QQQ, IWM, RSP, and DXY presets are convenience references. They are not learned, point-in-time validated, or guaranteed economically appropriate benchmarks.
    \item \textbf{Window dependence.} Relative-index basing, component fit/calibration splits, and own-fit context can change with requested history.
    \item \textbf{Market harmonization.} Currency, dividends, corporate actions, calendars, timezones, market hours, and stale-price risk are not jointly normalized.
    \item \textbf{Beta fragility.} A 20--60 observation slope can be outlier-sensitive and nonstationary. The standard-deviation and absolute-beta gates are engineering safeguards, not statistically selected thresholds. There are no uncertainty intervals.
    \item \textbf{Multiple views.} Fifteen coordinates, many possible pairs, two gap bases, many windows, and several scope projections create substantial multiple-comparison and researcher-degree-of-freedom risk.
    \item \textbf{No persistent shared Forms.} Each component dictionary is request-local. Cross-symbol Form identity, centroid matching, or transition comparison is intentionally absent.
    \item \textbf{Not multivariate.} Pair v1 has no basket, cross-sectional rank, factor decomposition, covariance shrinkage, or connectedness network.
    \item \textbf{Visual semantics.} Oscilloscope-like scope paths are two-dimensional projections. Loops are not detected cycles, attractors, lead--lag relations, or causal mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{Operational scale.} Large visible windows serialize 15 complete coordinate paths plus price context. Cache policy and response compression affect latency but do not change the mathematics.
    \item \textbf{Human-exposure evidence.} Bounded browser events distinguish a
    rendered result, a substantially visible Overview, opened Field/Audit
    panels, changed basis/coordinate, copied summary/live recipe, exported
    receipt, and same-session navigation to Secret Options. They are ordinary
    product telemetry, not an authenticated durable exposure ledger, and
    historical exposure cannot be reconstructed. The separate second-frame
    diagnostic is not evidence that the Overview was seen or useful.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Predeclared Validation and Extension Plan}

The present implementation is suitable for prospective instrumentation, not promotion into option ranking. A defensible next study should freeze the following before reading outcomes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a point-in-time comparator universe and mapping policy separating direct competitors, sector proxies, broad-cap-weighted and equal-weighted indexes, small-cap proxies, and macro references;
    \item provider, adjustment, currency, session, timezone, and completed-bar rules;
    \item pair start dates, visible windows, horizon grids, fit/calibration/evaluation fractions, and missingness thresholds;
    \item representation endpoints (support, stability, sensitivity), predictive endpoints, and separately cost-aware economic endpoints;
    \item robust, exponentially weighted, and shrinkage beta comparators selected before holdout;
    \item dependence-aware uncertainty and multiplicity correction across coordinates, pairs, and windows;
    \item an untouched prospective holdout, a durable authenticated exposure
    ledger distinct from product analytics, and retained probe receipts; and
    \item explicit promotion gates preserving current zero scanner and option-learning authority until incremental value and authority-boundary invariance are shown.
\end{enumerate}

Property-based randomized identity/swap tests, suffix-mutation tests for pair
context under a frozen split, expanded hash-tamper tests, and
missing-provenance tests remain near-term software work. Runtime response-schema
validation and a route probe are now implemented, but cold/concurrent
distributions, browser time-to-first-useful-summary, interaction latency,
pixel-diff baselines, manual screen-reader/color-vision review, and real-device
checks remain open. Basket fields, point-in-time peer selection,
cross-sectional ranking, and multivariate connectedness would be new model
families rather than silent Pair-v1 extensions and should receive new version
identifiers.

\section{Conclusion}

\Pair{} is best understood as a typed, ordered comparison operator. Its strongest properties are explicitness: same-recipe legs, exact key intersection, bilateral support, two separate coordinate bases, full-precision price context, strictly prior beta estimation, fixed failure semantics, a self-checking unkeyed compact receipt, and zero authority. Those properties make it suitable for inspection and prospective research. They do not make the chosen benchmark correct, the pair stationary, the gap predictive, or the output economically useful. Those questions remain empirical and must be answered under a frozen, dependence-aware, cost-aware protocol before any promotion.
